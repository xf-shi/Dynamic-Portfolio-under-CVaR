How to read CVaR here

This page reports the positive lower-tail wealth level, so a larger number means a safer bad-outcome average. Some notebook tables use the equivalent loss convention and print the same quantity with a minus sign—for example, loss CVaR −0.94.

Under the hood

Serious numerics, explained plainly.

The notebooks keep the full workflow visible: solve a feedback problem, tune the tail-risk threshold, simulate paths, and verify the resulting policy.

V

HJB solver

A finite-difference dynamic program computes how the best decision changes with time and state.

η

Golden η search

A one-dimensional golden-section search locates the Rockafellar threshold used to express CVaR.

λ

Multiplier bisection

An outer bisection raises or lowers the penalty until the requested tail floor is met.

MC

Path validation

Monte Carlo paths check terminal wealth, exposure, CVaR, and representative good and bad markets.

Which notebook should I open first?

Start with Complete_Market_Plots.ipynb. It has the smallest state space and makes the core CVaR trade-off easiest to see.

What does “active” mean?

The CVaR constraint affects the optimum and has a positive multiplier. “Inactive” means the configured constraint is loose enough that the unconstrained solution remains acceptable.

Why does average wealth fall under the active policy?

Tail protection is not free. The policy accepts less risky exposure—and therefore less expected return—to improve the average outcome within the worst five percent of terminal wealth.

Are the printed values guaranteed on every rerun?

No. They are the stored notebook outputs for the configured seeds and grids. Fresh Monte Carlo samples have ordinary sampling variation, and results can shift if you change discretization, seeds, tolerances, or model parameters.
